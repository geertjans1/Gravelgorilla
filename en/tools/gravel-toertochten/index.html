Riding gravel together, at your own pace

An organised gravel tour (in Dutch: toertocht) is the easiest way to discover new gravel tracks. A club plans a route, you sign up and ride at your own pace. There's usually a coffee stop along the way, and often soup or a beer afterwards. It's not a race: there are no results and nobody looks at your time.

How does it work?

Choose when you want to ride (this weekend, the next four weeks or a month) and where: tap a province or use your location to see rides within 25 to 200 km (as the crow flies). Then choose how far you want to ride. For each ride you see the distances, whether you follow arrows or a GPS route, the day price if known, and a link to the official page to register. With "Add to calendar" you put the ride in your own calendar.

Where do the rides come from?

  • Netherlands: the touring calendar of the NTFU (Dutch cycling federation) on fietssport.nl, gravel bike rides only.
  • Flanders: the recreational calendar of Cycling Vlaanderen, rides with a gravel route only.
  • Wallonia: the calendar of the FFBC, rides with a gravel route only.
  • Individual organisers: rides we found via NL Tour Rides, True Grit, Land van Grind en Zand and Grit, among others, and then checked with the organiser.
  • Germany: gravel tours just across the border that are listed on the Dutch calendar. In October 2026 the German federation calendar had no gravel tours close to the border yet.

Many rides for spring and summer 2027 aren't known yet: clubs often publish their dates only a few months ahead. That's why the list is updated regularly. The source pages are mostly in Dutch or French.

Tips for your first gravel tour

Pick a distance you'd also ride on a normal day: unpaved is harder than tarmac. Load the GPX onto your bike computer the night before, bring enough food and drink and check the organiser's website just before you leave. In winter the tracks are often muddy: with slightly lower tyre pressure and the right clothing you'll ride more comfortably.

Frequently asked questions

What is an organised gravel tour?

An organised ride on unpaved roads and tracks that you sign up for. You choose from a few distances, follow arrows or a GPS route and there's a coffee stop along the way. It's not a race: you ride at your own pace.

Do I need to be a member of a cycling federation or club?

No, anyone can take part. Members of the NTFU, Cycling Vlaanderen or the FFBC often get a discount and are sometimes insured. The price shown for a ride is the normal day price without membership.

When are most gravel tours held?

In the Netherlands mainly from October to March: many nature areas are quieter then and clubs hold their autumn and winter rides. In Flanders many tours in spring and summer also have a gravel route.

How do I add my own ride?

Use the form below the calendar. Fill in the name, date, start town and website of your ride. We check every submission first and then add the ride to the calendar for free.

Can I ride a gravel tour on a mountain bike?

Usually yes. A gravel route is fine on a mountain bike, you're just a bit slower on the paved parts. The other way round doesn't always work: on a gravel bike, avoid a ride's mountain bike route unless the organiser says it's suitable.
