    How to choose the right frame size

    "What size do I need?" It's the question every gravel rider asks at some point, and the answer in the shop is often "it depends". True, but on what? On your height, your inseam, your build and how you want to sit. This size finder turns those four things into a frame size, a saddle height and the stack and reach to look out for.

    How does it work?

    Set your height and inseam, tell it whether your upper body is rather short or long and how you want to sit. You immediately see which size fits, roughly how high your saddle should be and which stack and reach go with that size. Between two sizes? Then the gorilla says which he would choose, and why.

    Between two sizes: smaller or bigger?

    • Choose the smaller size if you have long legs for your height, if you like to sit upright and comfortably, or if you bikepack a lot and want a nimble bike. A longer seatpost and a slightly higher stem make it fit.
    • Choose the bigger size if you have a long upper body or long arms, if you want to sit sporty and stretched, or if you ride fast on quick gravel roads a lot. A bigger size is more stable in a straight line.

    Stack and reach: the numbers that really matter

    Frame sizes like "54" or "M" differ per brand. That's why bike shops look at stack and reach. Stack is how high the handlebar sits (from the centre of the bottom bracket to the top of the head tube). Reach is how far forward the handlebar sits. A higher stack and shorter reach are more comfortable; a lower stack and longer reach sportier. Comparing two bikes? Put those two numbers side by side.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which gravel bike frame size do I need for my height?

    As a guideline: up to about 162 cm size XS, 160 to 170 cm size S, 168 to 178 cm size M, 176 to 186 cm size L, 184 to 194 cm size XL and above that XXL. The ranges overlap: if you're in an overlap, your inseam and build decide which size fits better. The size finder works it out for you.

    How do I measure my inseam?

    Stand barefoot with your back against a wall, feet slightly apart. Clamp a thick book between your legs and pull it up firmly, like a saddle. Measure from the top of the book to the floor. Do it twice and take the average.

    How high should my saddle be?

    A well-known rule of thumb is your inseam times 0.883, measured from the centre of the bottom bracket to the top of the saddle. With an 83 cm inseam that's about 73 cm. On the bike you check it like this: with your heel on the pedal at the bottom, your leg should be just straight.

    I'm between two sizes, what now?

    Long legs, a short upper body or a preference for sitting upright: choose the smaller size. A long upper body, long arms or a sporty position: choose the bigger one. With the stem and seatpost you can then adjust a few more centimetres. Best of all is always a test ride on both sizes.

    Is the size of a gravel bike different from a road bike?

    Often you ride the same size on a gravel bike as on your road bike, but gravel bikes usually have a higher front (more stack) and a slightly shorter reach. That makes you sit a bit more upright. So compare the stack and reach in the geometry chart, not just the size number.
