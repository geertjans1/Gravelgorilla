Never forget anything again

The evening before you leave, a pile of gear on the floor and that nagging feeling you're forgetting something. Usually it's the charger or the quick link, and you only notice on day two. This packing list thinks along with you: it adapts to your trip, calculates the weight and remembers what you've already packed.

How does it work?

Choose how long you're away, in which season, where you sleep, whether you can easily buy food on the way and whether you cook. The list grows or shrinks with it. Tick off what's in your bags: your ticks are saved on your own device, even if you open the page again later. Done? Copy the list to your notes or print it.

Packing lighter: the big three

Most of the weight is almost always in three things: your tent, your sleeping bag and your sleeping mat. Together they can easily save a kilo. After that it helps to take a critical look at everything you take "just in case". Two items with the same job (a fleece and a down jacket) are often double.

How to spread the weight

  • Frame bag: heavy things like tools, food and the power bank. Low and central steers most calmly.
  • Saddle bag: clothing and your sleeping bag. Light but bulky.
  • Handlebar roll: tent or tarp and the sleeping mat. Not too heavy, or your bike steers sluggishly.
  • Top tube bag: snacks, phone and sunscreen: everything you want to grab on the go without stopping.

Frequently asked questions

What do I take on a bikepacking trip?

The basics are always: bags, something to sleep in, spare clothing, rain gear, food and drink, a repair kit, lights with a power bank and a first-aid kit. How much you need depends on the length, the season and whether you sleep in a tent or a hotel. The packing list works it out for your trip.

How much does bikepacking gear weigh?

For a trip of a few nights with a tent, with light gear you usually end up at 7 to 10 kilos of luggage. If you sleep in hotels or B&Bs, 3 to 5 kilos can do. In winter and on remote routes it quickly gets more, because of a warmer sleeping bag and extra food and water.

Which bags do I need for bikepacking?

Most riders use a saddle bag, a frame bag and a handlebar roll, plus a small top tube bag for snacks. For longer or remote trips, fork cages are added for extra water or gear. You don't need a rack.

Which tools and repair items do I take?

A pump, one or two inner tubes (even if you ride tubeless), tubeless plugs, tyre levers, a multitool with chain breaker, a quick link, chain oil, zip ties and duct tape. Going for a week or longer? Also take spare brake pads and a derailleur hanger that fits your frame.

Are my ticks stored anywhere?

Only on your own device, in your browser. Nothing goes to Gravel Gorilla. If you open the packing list on another device or in a private window, you start with an empty list. With the "Clear ticks" button you start again.
