    How to choose the right gravel tyre

    Tyres are the cheapest upgrade that changes your bike the most. The same gravel bike feels like a nimble all-road racer with a fast semi-slick, and like a small mountain bike with a knobbly tyre. But with dozens of brands, treads and widths you can't see the trail for the knobs. This finder lines them up for you.

    How does it work?

    Slide what surface you usually ride, how you ride and what weather you expect. Tell it how wide a tyre your frame takes and what matters most to you. The finder first advises a tyre width and a type of tread, and then picks from over 50 popular gravel tyres the ones that fit best, with a match score and the reasons. With one click you then calculate the right tyre pressure.

    Width: wider is often better

    A wider tyre runs at lower pressure, which gives more grip and comfort on rough tracks, without noticeably slowing you down. On tarmac and hardpack, 35 to 40 mm is fine. On mixed and loose gravel, 40 to 45 mm often feels best. In sand or with bikepacking luggage, 45 to 50 mm is no luxury. Do keep a few millimetres of clearance in your frame, or the tyre will rub as soon as mud sticks to it.

    Tread: from slick to mud tyre

    • Slick and semi-slick: a smooth or fine centre strip. Rolls fastest on tarmac, cycle paths and firm gravel roads.
    • All-round: small, dense knobs. Fast on the firm sections and still grip on loose gravel. The best choice for most riders.
    • Aggressive tread: taller knobs with sturdy shoulders for loose, rough and rocky terrain.
    • Mud tread: widely spaced knobs that clean themselves in sand, mud and wet forest.

    In doubt between two tyres? Choose the one that suits the hardest surface you regularly ride. You hardly lose speed on the firm sections; you miss grip on the loose sections straight away.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which gravel tyre is best?

    There's no best tyre for everyone: it depends on your surface, your riding style and the weather. For riders on varied surfaces, an all-round tyre of 40 to 45 mm is usually the best choice. If you ride a lot of tarmac and hardpack, a semi-slick is faster; in sand and mud an aggressive tread helps more. The finder works it out for your situation.

    How wide should my gravel tyre be?

    On tarmac and hardpack 35 to 40 mm is enough, on mixed and loose gravel 40 to 45 mm often feels best, and in sand or with luggage 45 to 50 mm is nice. Always check how wide a tyre your frame takes and keep a few millimetres of clearance for mud.

    Which gravel tyre for sand?

    In loose sand a wide tyre floats better than a narrow one. Choose 45 mm or wider if your frame allows, with an all-round or aggressive tread, and ride with a bit less pressure. Set the surface in the finder to "Sand & mud" for advice.

    What's the difference between a semi-slick and an all-round tyre?

    A semi-slick has a smooth or very fine centre strip with knobs on the sides: fast on firm ground, with grip in corners. An all-round tyre has small knobs across the whole width: a bit slower on tarmac, but more grip on loose gravel and wet tracks.

    Where does the data come from?

    Widths and weights come from the manufacturers (700c, as of October 2026). The tread type, scores and price class are Gravel Gorilla's own estimate. Many tyres link to the rolling resistance test on bicyclerollingresistance.com, where you can see the measured rolling resistance.
