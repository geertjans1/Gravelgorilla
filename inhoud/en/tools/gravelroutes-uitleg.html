"Where can I ride gravel around here?" It may well be the most asked gravel question. In the Netherlands and Flanders many forest tracks and sandy roads are not open to cyclists, and not every unpaved path on the map is allowed. That's why the gorilla lists routes here that you can simply ride: official, signposted gravel routes and GPX routes from reliable sources.

How does it work?

Tap a province on the map, or choose a country, a distance and whether you'd rather follow signs or a GPX file. You immediately see which routes there are. Each route links to its source: there you find the start, the signposting or the GPX file, and whether you need a permit.

Signposted or GPX?

  • Signposted: you follow posts or arrows, often the green signs of the official mountain bike routes. Many of those routes have a special gravel version without narrow singletracks. Handy if you ride without a bike computer.
  • GPX route: you download the file and put it on your bike computer, Komoot or your phone. That way you can also ride longer tours through several areas.

Rules on gravel

Stay on the route: riding off the marked trails is not allowed in many nature areas. Greet walkers and horse riders, pass them slowly and close gates behind you. Some routes ask for a small contribution or a permit (vignet) for maintenance. And ride your first gravel route with the profile sticker: then you see exactly what's still to come.
