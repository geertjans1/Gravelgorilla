"What size do I need?" It's the question every gravel rider asks at some point, and the answer in the shop is often "it depends". True, but on what? On your height, your inseam, your build and how you want to sit. This size finder turns those four things into a frame size, a saddle height and the stack and reach to look out for.

How does it work?

Set your height and inseam, tell it whether your upper body is rather short or long and how you want to sit. You immediately see which size fits, roughly how high your saddle should be and which stack and reach go with that size. Between two sizes? Then the gorilla says which he would choose, and why.

Between two sizes: smaller or bigger?

  • Choose the smaller size if you have long legs for your height, if you like to sit upright and comfortably, or if you bikepack a lot and want a nimble bike. A longer seatpost and a slightly higher stem make it fit.
  • Choose the bigger size if you have a long upper body or long arms, if you want to sit sporty and stretched, or if you ride fast on quick gravel roads a lot. A bigger size is more stable in a straight line.

Stack and reach: the numbers that really matter

Frame sizes like "54" or "M" differ per brand. That's why bike shops look at stack and reach. Stack is how high the handlebar sits (from the centre of the bottom bracket to the top of the head tube). Reach is how far forward the handlebar sits. A higher stack and shorter reach are more comfortable; a lower stack and longer reach sportier. Comparing two bikes? Put those two numbers side by side.
