Too much tyre pressure and you rattle over every root. You lose grip as soon as it gets loose and every bump goes straight through your hands. Too little and you pinch-flat yourself, or you drag a soft, slow tyre along. The difference between right and wrong is usually a few tenths of a bar. Yet hardly anyone actually stops to work it out.

Our tyre pressure calculator does the maths for you. Enter your weight, including luggage, the width of your tyre (from a fast 28 mm to a wide 55 mm), your wheel size, valve type and the surface you ride on. You immediately get advice per wheel in both psi and bar. Your front tyre automatically gets about 10% less than your rear, because that gives just enough grip in corners without the front washing out. Outside the usual range for gravel tyres? Then the tool warns you. Better that than leaving the car park with far too much or too little air.

On sandy forest trails you need that advice more than anywhere. From smooth "champagne gravel" to a deep strip of loose sand in a dry summer: every surface asks for a different pressure. The same trails can also be full of roots and hidden holes, which punish a tyre that's too soft with a hit against the rim. Set the surface to "rough", enter your tyre width and weight, and the calculator balances that contrast for you. Still riding with inner tubes? Then the tool adds an extra correction, because a pinch flat from a hard hit on a root is not a pleasant break in your ride.

Set it before you leave, fine-tune by feel in the first kilometres, and you no longer have to guess on the way.
