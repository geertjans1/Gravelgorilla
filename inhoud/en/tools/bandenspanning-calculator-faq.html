What is a good tyre pressure for a gravel bike?

That depends on your weight, the width of your tyre and the surface. As an example: for a 75 kg rider on 40 mm tubeless tyres on mixed surfaces, the calculator comes to about 1.8 bar front and 2.0 bar rear. Heavier, narrower tyres or inner tubes mean more pressure; wider tyres or loose sand mean less.

Why should my front tyre be softer than my rear?

Your rear wheel carries most of your weight. A front tyre with about 10 percent less pressure gives more grip in corners and more comfort in your hands, without the front washing out.

Tubeless or inner tube: does it matter for pressure?

Yes. With an inner tube the calculator adds an extra correction. A tyre that's too soft with an inner tube pinch-flats more easily: a puncture because the tube gets squeezed against the rim, for example when you hit a tree root.

Which tyres does the calculator work for?

For tyres from 28 to 55 mm wide, on 700c and 650b wheels, for riders from 45 to 130 kg including gear. You choose the surface (smooth, mixed or rough) and whether you ride tubeless or with inner tubes. You get the advice in bar and psi.

How do I measure my tyre pressure?

With a pump with a good gauge, or with a separate digital gauge. Tyres slowly lose air, especially tubeless ones. So check the pressure before every ride and fine-tune by feel after the first kilometres.
