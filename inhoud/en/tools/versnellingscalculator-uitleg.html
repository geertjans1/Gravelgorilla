Halfway up a steep gravel climb, with luggage, and you're already in your lowest gear. That's when you want to know: should I have taken a smaller chainring? Or a cassette with a bigger sprocket? This calculator works it out before you buy anything. You see how light your lowest gear is, how fast you can still pedal downhill and, most importantly, how steep a climb you can still ride.

How does it work?

Choose your chainring and your cassette (A) and optionally another setup to compare (B). Enter your wheel size and tyre width, your cadence uphill, your weight with bike and luggage and the power you can hold uphill. Per setup you see all your gears in a row, the speed in each gear and the steepest climb you can still ride seated in your lowest gear.

1× or 2×?

  • 1× (one chainring): simple, light and less maintenance. With a large cassette (10–44 or bigger) you have enough range for most gravel rides. The jumps between gears are bigger, though.
  • 2× (two chainrings): more range and smaller jumps. Nice if you also ride fast on tarmac and do steep climbs with luggage.

What is a good lowest gear?

For flat gravel rides almost anything is fine. In the Ardennes, hilly regions or with bikepacking luggage, you want a lowest gear lighter than 1:1: the chainring smaller than the biggest sprocket at the back, for example 40 front and 44 or more at the rear. A rule of thumb: if in your lowest gear at your normal cadence you can ride 8 to 10 percent seated, you'll manage most climbs. Steeper bits you do standing or at a lower cadence.
