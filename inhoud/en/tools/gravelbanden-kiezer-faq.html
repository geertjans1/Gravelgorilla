Which gravel tyre is best?

There's no best tyre for everyone: it depends on your surface, your riding style and the weather. For riders on varied surfaces, an all-round tyre of 40 to 45 mm is usually the best choice. If you ride a lot of tarmac and hardpack, a semi-slick is faster; in sand and mud an aggressive tread helps more. The finder works it out for your situation.

How wide should my gravel tyre be?

On tarmac and hardpack 35 to 40 mm is enough, on mixed and loose gravel 40 to 45 mm often feels best, and in sand or with luggage 45 to 50 mm is nice. Always check how wide a tyre your frame takes and keep a few millimetres of clearance for mud.

Which gravel tyre for sand?

In loose sand a wide tyre floats better than a narrow one. Choose 45 mm or wider if your frame allows, with an all-round or aggressive tread, and ride with a bit less pressure. Set the surface in the finder to "Sand & mud" for advice.

What's the difference between a semi-slick and an all-round tyre?

A semi-slick has a smooth or very fine centre strip with knobs on the sides: fast on firm ground, with grip in corners. An all-round tyre has small knobs across the whole width: a bit slower on tarmac, but more grip on loose gravel and wet tracks.

Where does the data come from?

Widths and weights come from the manufacturers (700c, as of October 2026). The tread type, scores and price class are Gravel Gorilla's own estimate. Many tyres link to the rolling resistance test on bicyclerollingresistance.com, where you can see the measured rolling resistance.
