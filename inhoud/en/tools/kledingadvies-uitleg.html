It's half past eight and you're at the front door. It's 7 degrees, there's a fresh wind and this afternoon it'll be 14. Long tights or knee warmers? Jacket or gilet? Leave too warm and you're soaked in sweat after fifteen minutes, then freeze on the descent. Leave too cold and you never warm up in those first hours. That's why there's this clothing advice tool.

How does it work?

Enter the temperature, or let the tool fetch the weather forecast for your location and start time. Tell it how long and how hard you'll ride and whether you get cold or warm quickly. The tool works out how warm it feels on the bike: wind, rain, an easy pace and wet mud make it colder, race pace makes it warmer. Based on that you get what to wear per body part, what goes in your back pocket and a few tips for this ride.

Why a "feels like" temperature?

On the bike you make your own wind. At 10 degrees with a strong wind it quickly feels like 6, especially if you ride easy or the road is wet. The other way round, 10 degrees at race pace is almost pleasant. By calculating with the feels-like temperature, the advice is far more accurate than with just the number on your weather app.

Three golden rules

  • Start a little cold. After ten minutes of pedalling you're up to temperature. If it already feels nice at the front door, you're wearing too much.
  • Choose layers you can take off. Arm warmers, knee warmers and a gilet fit in your back pocket. That way you stay well dressed if it warms up on the way.
  • Hands and feet first. Cold fingers and toes spoil a ride faster than anything. In doubt? Take the warmer gloves.

The advice is a guideline: everyone is different. If after a few rides you notice you're always too warm or too cold, choose a different setting at "How are you?".
