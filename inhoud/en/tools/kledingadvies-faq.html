What should I wear cycling at 10 degrees?

At 10 degrees without wind or rain the tool advises: bib shorts with leg warmers or thin bib tights, a base layer with a long-sleeved jersey (or short sleeves with arm warmers) and a gilet, thin long-finger gloves, wool socks and a buff or headband over your ears. If it's windy or raining it feels colder, so dress warmer.

What is the feels-like temperature on the bike?

On the bike you make your own wind. Wind, rain, an easy pace and wet mud make it colder than the number on your weather app; race pace makes it warmer. That's why the tool calculates with a "feels like" temperature and picks your clothing for that.

Where does the weather forecast come from?

From Open-Meteo, a free weather service. You enter a place, date and start time, and the tool fetches the temperature, chance of rain and wind for the hours of your ride. You don't need an account.

What do I take in my back pocket?

That depends on the weather and your ride. Often it's a gilet, a buff, arm warmers and, if showers are possible, a packable rain jacket. On long rides in the rain, dry spare gloves are nice. The tool lists it for you, and you can copy everything as a packing list.

How do I avoid getting too warm or too cold?

Start feeling slightly cold: after ten minutes of pedalling you're up to temperature. Choose layers you can take off, such as arm warmers, knee warmers and a gilet. In doubt about hands and feet? Go warmer; cold fingers and toes spoil a ride the fastest.
