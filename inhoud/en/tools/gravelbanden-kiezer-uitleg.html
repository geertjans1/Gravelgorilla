Tyres are the cheapest upgrade that changes your bike the most. The same gravel bike feels like a nimble all-road racer with a fast semi-slick, and like a small mountain bike with a knobbly tyre. But with dozens of brands, treads and widths you can't see the trail for the knobs. This finder lines them up for you.

How does it work?

Slide what surface you usually ride, how you ride and what weather you expect. Tell it how wide a tyre your frame takes and what matters most to you. The finder first advises a tyre width and a type of tread, and then picks from over 40 popular gravel tyres the ones that fit best, with a match score and the reasons. With one click you then calculate the right tyre pressure.

Width: wider is often better

A wider tyre runs at lower pressure, which gives more grip and comfort on rough tracks, without noticeably slowing you down. On tarmac and hardpack, 35 to 40 mm is fine. On mixed and loose gravel, 40 to 45 mm often feels best. In sand or with bikepacking luggage, 45 to 50 mm is no luxury. Do keep a few millimetres of clearance in your frame, or the tyre will rub as soon as mud sticks to it.

Tread: from slick to mud tyre

  • Slick and semi-slick: a smooth or fine centre strip. Rolls fastest on tarmac, cycle paths and firm gravel roads.
  • All-round: small, dense knobs. Fast on the firm sections and still grip on loose gravel. The best choice for most riders.
  • Aggressive tread: taller knobs with sturdy shoulders for loose, rough and rocky terrain.
  • Mud tread: widely spaced knobs that clean themselves in sand, mud and wet forest.

In doubt between two tyres? Choose the one that suits the hardest surface you regularly ride. You hardly lose speed on the firm sections; you miss grip on the loose sections straight away.
